## Page 5 — Core B (4 Columns)

**Visual Note:** Forest green background. White column headers. Ivory body text. Gold accent icons or numbers at the top of each column. Equal column width.

**Section Header:** Four Steps To Shape Your Carbon Story

**Column 1 — Step 1: Anchor in Reality**
Begin with what you have already done. Specific actions — switching to renewable energy, cutting business travel, changing suppliers — are more believable than broad claims about commitment.

**Column 2 — Step 2: Speak in Plain Numbers**
Use clear, everyday language to share your results. Instead of technical figures, say "we cut our energy use by a third" or "we removed 500 tonnes of carbon from our supply chain."

**Column 3 — Step 3: Show the Journey**
Share where you started, where you are now, and where you are headed. A visible before-and-after builds credibility. Audiences trust brands that show effort, not just outcomes.

**Column 4 — Step 4: Connect to Impact**
Link your progress to something your audience already cares about — cleaner air, healthier communities, a more stable future. Make the benefit personal, not just environmental.

## Page 6 — Core C (2 Columns)

**Visual Note:** White background. Left column has forest green header block. Right column has gold header block. Body text in dark charcoal. Clean, professional layout.

**Section Header:** Messaging That Builds Trust

**Column 1 — What To Say**
Be specific and honest. Use the language your audience uses, not the language of internal reports. Highlight real milestones — a reduction target reached, a supplier partnership formed, a product reformulated.

Avoid vague language like "we are committed to a greener future." Instead, say "we cut our warehouse energy use by 40% this year."

**Column 2 — Where To Say It**
Your carbon story belongs on every major channel — your website, your social media, your sales materials, and your investor presentations. Consistency builds recognition. Repetition builds belief.

Work with Estelle Darcy's research on what your audience already knows and cares about. Tailor the depth of the message by channel — shorter for social, deeper for long-form content.
