## Page 5 — Core B (4 Columns)

**Visual Note:** Deep teal background. White column headers. Ivory body text. Gold accent markers at column tops. Bold, professional grid.

**Section Header:** Four Strategic Moves for Climate-Era Marketing

**Column 1 — Move 1: Acknowledge Reality**
Do not pretend global warming is not part of your brand context. Audiences notice when brands stay silent on issues that affect their daily lives. Acknowledge the reality, then show what your brand is doing about it.

**Column 2 — Move 2: Say What Is True**
Estelle Darcy's audience research shows that people are more skeptical of brand climate messages than almost any other category. Earn their trust by saying only what is verifiable. One honest claim outweighs ten enthusiastic ones.

**Column 3 — Move 3: Lead with Solutions**
Every climate-era brand message works better when it is forward-facing. Show what your product, service, or operation is doing to be part of a better outcome. Give your audience something to believe in, not just something to worry about.

**Column 4 — Move 4: Be Consistent**
A climate-era brand message must show up everywhere — your packaging, your social media, your team communications, and your advertising. Inconsistency is the fastest way to lose the trust you have worked to build.

## Page 6 — Core C (2 Columns)

**Visual Note:** Ivory background. Left column with deep teal header block. Right column with gold header block. Body in dark charcoal. Structured and practical.

**Section Header:** Messaging That Holds Up Over Time

**Column 1 — Tone and Language**
The tone for climate-era marketing is honest, calm, and forward-looking. Avoid alarm — it exhausts audiences. Avoid optimism that feels unrealistic — it loses trust. Find the space between: this is real, we know it, and here is what we are doing.

Use plain language throughout. Replace "carbon-neutral pathway" with "plan to remove more carbon than we produce." Replace "climate resilience" with "ready for what comes next." Your audience will thank you for the clarity.

**Column 2 — Long-Term Brand Build**
Global warming is not a campaign moment — it is a permanent part of your brand context. The most effective marketing teams treat climate messaging as a long-term brand investment, not a one-quarter push.

Adeline Palmerston's framework recommends anchoring your climate message in three consistent proof points that you build on over time — rather than refreshing your story with every news cycle. Stability earns trust. Reactive messaging loses it.
