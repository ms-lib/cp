## Page 3 — Context (3 Columns)

**Headline:** Three Elements Of A Strong Leadership Vision

**Column 1 — Direction**
**Subhead:** Know Where You Are Going
A strong vision tells the team exactly where they are going. It removes guesswork, aligns decisions, and gives every action a clear point of reference that leaders can return to again and again.

**Column 2 — Meaning**
**Subhead:** Make The Work Matter
People need to know why the work matters. A vision that carries meaning gives the team a reason to push through challenges — and reminds them that what they do has real impact beyond the day-to-day.

**Column 3 — Momentum**
**Subhead:** Keep The Energy Moving
The best visions create energy. They inspire people to move, take initiative, and bring their best. A vision with momentum does not just describe the future — it pulls the team toward it.

## Page 4 — Core A (1 Column)

**Headline:** Why Vision Is A Leader's First Responsibility

**Body:**
A leader without a clear vision is just managing the present. The most important thing a leader does is define where the team is going before deciding how to get there. Vision gives strategy its meaning — without it, even the best plans feel hollow and hard to follow.

---

## Page 5 — Core B (4 Columns)

**Headline:** Four Steps To Align Vision With Strategy

**Column 1**
**Step 01 — Define**
Start with a clear, simple statement of where you are going. Make it bold enough to inspire — and specific enough to guide real decisions every day.

**Column 2**
**Step 02 — Connect**
Make sure your strategy reflects what the team believes in. When the plan matches the values, people follow it not just because they have to — but because they want to.

**Column 3**
**Step 03 — Break It Down**
Translate the vision into phases, milestones, and priorities. Give the team a clear path so they know what to do today, this quarter, and this year.

**Column 4**
**Step 04 — Review**
A great vision stays relevant because leaders revisit it. Check your strategy against your vision regularly. Adjust what is not working — without losing sight of where you are going.
