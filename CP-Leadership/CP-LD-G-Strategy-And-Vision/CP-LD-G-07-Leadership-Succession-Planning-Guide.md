## Page 3 — Context (3 Columns)

**Headline:** Three Pillars Of Succession Planning

**Column 1**
**Identify**
The first step in succession planning is seeing clearly who is ready to grow. Organizations that identify future leaders early — based on performance, values, and potential — build a pipeline that is strong long before it is ever needed.

**Column 2**
**Develop**
Identifying future leaders is not enough. They need active, intentional development — coaching, stretch opportunities, and honest feedback. Development is where potential becomes capability, and where leaders learn by doing, not just observing.

**Column 3**
**Transition**
The moment of leadership transition is where succession planning is either proven or exposed. A well-managed transition protects continuity, supports both outgoing and incoming leaders, and keeps the organization moving without losing momentum or trust.

## Page 5 — Core B (3 Columns)

**Headline:** Three Succession Planning Phases

**Column 1**
**Phase 1 — Assess**
Start by mapping the current leadership landscape. Identify key roles, review high-potential team members, and document the skills and experiences the next generation of leaders will need to lead Liceria & Co. effectively.

**Column 2**
**Phase 2 — Develop**
Create tailored development plans for future leaders. Pair them with mentors, expose them to cross-functional work, and give them the kind of real-world challenges that build genuine leadership readiness — not just technical skills.

**Column 3**
**Phase 3 — Transition**
Plan transitions before they are needed. Define what a successful handover looks like, prepare both the incoming and outgoing leaders, and communicate in a way that builds confidence and trust across the organization.
