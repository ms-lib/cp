## Page 3 — Context (2 Columns)

**Headline:** Two Ways Leaders Shape Culture

**Column 1**
**What They Say**
Every time a leader speaks — in a meeting, in feedback, in a casual conversation — they send a signal about what the organization values. The words leaders choose shape what the team believes is important, what is safe to say, and what kind of behavior the organization actually rewards. Language is one of the most powerful tools a leader has.

**Column 2**
**What They Do**
Actions carry more weight than words. The team watches how leaders behave under pressure, how they treat people when things go wrong, and whether they follow through on what they say. When behavior matches values, culture grows strong. When it does not, trust breaks down — and that is the hardest thing to rebuild.

## Page 4 — Core A (1 Column)

**Headline:** You Shape Culture Even When You Don't Mean To

**Body:**
Culture is built in moments no one plans for — a leader who stays calm in a crisis or admits a mistake openly. The team is always watching, drawing conclusions about what the organization truly values. That is an opportunity to lead with intention every single day.

## Page 5 — Core B (4 Columns)

**Headline:** Four Cultural Levers Leaders Control

**Column 1**
**Lever 01 — Tone in the Room**
How a leader enters a conversation — relaxed or tense, open or closed — sets the emotional temperature for everyone else. A calm, open leader creates a safe space where people do their best thinking.

**Column 2**
**Lever 02 — Who Gets Recognized**
Who a leader praises tells the team what is valued. If recognition goes to the loudest voice, that is the culture you build. Recognize the behaviors you want to see more of — and the team will follow.

**Column 3**
**Lever 03 — How Mistakes Are Handled**
Teams learn the most from how their leaders respond to failure. Leaders who treat mistakes as learning moments build cultures of growth. Leaders who punish failure build cultures of silence.

**Column 4**
**Lever 04 — What Gets Decided**
Every decision a leader makes reflects their values — or exposes the gap between what they say and what they do. Consistent, values-based decisions are one of the strongest culture-builders a leader has.
