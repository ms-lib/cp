## Page 3 — Context (3 Columns)

**Headline:** Three Questions Every Leadership Vision Must Answer

**Column 1**
**Where Are We Going?**
A vision must name a clear destination. Not a vague direction — but a real place the team is working toward. The clearer the answer, the easier every future decision becomes for the whole team.

**Column 2**
**Why Does It Matter?**
A vision without purpose is just a target. The strongest visions explain why the destination matters — to the team, to the people they serve, and to the broader world they are part of.

**Column 3**
**How Will We Get There Together?**
Vision is not a solo plan. It must reflect how the team will work, grow, and move forward as one. A shared vision becomes a shared commitment — and that is what drives real results.

## Page 5 — Core B (4 Columns)

**Headline:** Four Steps To Build A Clear Leadership Vision

**Column 1**
**Step 01 — Reflect First**
Before you write anything, ask yourself what you believe in as a leader. What do you want the team to feel, achieve, and become? Start from the inside out.

**Column 2**
**Step 02 — Name the Destination**
Write your vision in one or two clear sentences. Make it specific enough to guide decisions — and bold enough to inspire the people who will work toward it every day.

**Column 3**
**Step 03 — Share It Early**
Do not wait for your vision to be perfect. Share it with trusted leaders and team members. Their response will tell you whether it connects — and help you make it sharper.

**Column 4**
**Step 04 — Live It Consistently**
A vision is only real when the leader models it every day. Bring it into meetings, decisions, and feedback. The team will believe it when they see it in your actions — not just your words.
