## Page 3 — Context (3 Columns)

**Section Title:**
Three Retrospective Questions

**Column 1 — What Worked**
**Column Heading:** What Worked

This question uncovers what to protect. The practices, habits, and decisions that served us well this year deserve to be recognized — and repeated. When we name what worked, we make it more likely that it continues. This is not just celebration; it is institutional memory.

**Column 2 — What Didn't Work**
**Column Heading:** What Didn't Work

This question creates the most value when answered honestly. What slowed us down? What decisions, in hindsight, should have gone differently? What gaps appeared between what we planned and what we delivered? Honest answers here are gifts — they save future time, effort, and energy.

**Column 3 — What We'll Do Next**
**Column Heading:** What We'll Do Next

This question turns reflection into action. It takes the insights from the first two questions and converts them into intention. What will we start doing, stop doing, or do differently? The answer to this question is where the retrospective earns its value.

## Page 4 — Core A (1 Column)

**Section Title:**
Why Leadership Teams Need to Reflect

**Body Paragraph:**
Moving fast is valuable. But moving fast without looking back means repeating the same mistakes and missing the same opportunities. Reflection is how leadership teams get smarter over time. A retrospective done well does not slow a team down — it gives the team a better chance at moving in the right direction.

---

## Page 5 — Core B (3 Columns)

**Section Title:**
Three Retrospective Phases

**Column 1 — Look Back**
**Column Heading:** Look Back

Before evaluating what happened, the team needs to see the year clearly. What were the key moments? What were the decisions that shaped outcomes? What were the moments that tested us? This phase is about building a shared picture of the year before drawing conclusions about it.

**Column 2 — Evaluate Honestly**
**Column Heading:** Evaluate Honestly

With a shared picture in place, the team asks the hard questions. What in that picture reflects strong leadership? What reflects choices we'd make differently today? This phase works only when every voice in the room is heard and no one's observation is dismissed.

**Column 3 — Commit to Action**
**Column Heading:** Commit to Action

The retrospective ends with decisions, not observations. The team chooses the top two or three changes to carry forward. These commitments are documented, assigned to owners, and revisited at the next review cycle. Without this phase, reflection is just storytelling.

## Page 8 — Emotional Peak (1 Column)

**Section Title:**
The Courage to Look Honestly

**Body Paragraph:**
Not every team takes the time to ask what didn't work. It requires a level of trust and honesty that is not easy to build — and even harder to sustain. When a leadership team can sit together, name what fell short, and agree on what to do next without losing confidence in each other, that is a team worth being part of. That kind of honesty is one of the most powerful things leadership can model for an organization.
